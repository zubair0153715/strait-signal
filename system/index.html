How detection works (activates with live AIS)

AIS-gap event

One vessel's consecutive position reports inside the corridor are separated by more than 6 hours (default, tunable). Consistent with AIS switched off, a receiver gap, or spoofing. Never proof of wrongdoing.

STS candidate

Two vessels stay within 0.5 nautical miles, both at ≤ 2 knots, continuously for ≥ 30 minutes (defaults, tunable). Consistent with a ship-to-ship transfer, joint anchorage, bunkering, or coincidence. Not proof of a transfer and not a sanctions finding.

Human review required

Every detector output is labelled candidate. Before any alert is sent or published: check the raw messages, check against satellite / port data, and never label a vessel "sanctioned" without an official sanctions-list match. See README → Legal red lines.

Pipeline: AISStream (needs free key) → pipeline/aisstream_client.py → data/ais_raw.jsonl → pipeline/detect.py → data/detected_events.json. Detector logic is unit-tested on labelled-synthetic fixtures (pipeline/test_detect.py).

Coverage & honesty notes

  • Free terrestrial AIS (AISStream) has regional gaps and latency; open-ocean satellite AIS is a paid layer this demo does not have.
  • Replay coordinates for H1 come from a single public source (TankerTrackers.com via Lokmat Times) and were not independently verified.
  • The "shadow fleet" description of ADALYNN is an unverified media characterization. This demo makes no sanctions claim.
  • GPS/electronic interference near Hormuz is reported context for June 2025, not a proven cause of the H1 collision (assessed as a navigational incident).
